# OEM timelines: Apple, Google, Samsung (AR/VR/XR glasses & headsets)

Compiled 2026-10-06. Scope: past product releases, announced/reported future products, camera/autofocus relevance, release cadence, and cross-links to the actuator/tunable-lens suppliers in this project (poLight, Q Tech, CML, Optotune, Alps Alpine, LG Innotek, SEMCO). Everything below is dated and sourced; where a date comes from a leak or an analyst note it is labelled as such. One source-quality warning up front: **vr.org** published a Google I/O 2026 "recap" (2026-05-19) with specific ship dates/prices ("Oct 14", "$799", "Pixel Buds Sight") and then appended a correction on 2026-09-23 saying those details were "inaccurate" and never announced by Google (https://vr.org/articles/google-io-2026-android-xr-glasses-ship-date-recap). vr.org items are therefore used only where a second outlet corroborates.

Context from the project brief that matters for reading the OEM timelines: poLight's qualification/"design-in" customer is described by poLight as a "Top Tier U.S. Consumer Electronics OEM"; a 2025-10-13 purchase order was explicitly "to support the Design of a TLens Camera for AR Applications" (https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025-10-13:newsml_MFN1YGcq2:0-polight-asa-receives-purchase-order-from-a-top-tier-u-s-consumer-electronics-oem-to-support-the-design-of-a-tlens-camera-for-ar-applications/), and a follow-on order says deliveries "will likely occur second half of 2026" (https://www.polight.com/mfn_news/polight-asa-announces-follow-on-tlens-purchase-order-supporting-top-tier-consumer-oem-qualification-program-2/). Of the three OEMs here, only Apple and Google are U.S. companies; Samsung is Korean but is the hardware builder of Google's first Android XR glasses (see Google/Samsung cross-links).

## 1. Apple

### 1.1 Profile
Apple sells one XR product line, Apple Vision Pro (announced 2023-06-05, shipped in the U.S. 2024-02-02, refreshed with the M5 chip 2025-10-22), a 12-camera/5-sensor headset running visionOS, which Apple updates on an annual June-announce/September-release cadence. Apple has no shipping glasses; Bloomberg (Mark Gurman) and TF Securities (Ming-Chi Kuo) report a display-less camera/audio "AI glasses" product (codename N50, chip project N401) now targeted for a WWDC June-2027 unveil and late-2027 shipment, after slipping from an end-2026 target. A cheaper/lighter "Vision Air" headset (reported for 2027) was paused in October 2025 to move engineers onto the glasses; display (waveguide) glasses are reported for 2029 and a revamped headset (N224) for late 2028/early 2029. Apple is relevant to the project because it is the most-cited alternative to Meta as a "top-tier U.S. consumer-electronics OEM", because N50 has two cameras (one capture, one computer-vision) whose configuration was reportedly still undecided in July 2026, and because Apple's component qualification cycle (prototype builds with overseas suppliers from late 2025 for a late-2027 product) overlaps the 2025–2026 TLens qualification orders.

### 1.4 Camera / autofocus relevance
- Vision Pro: 12 outward/inward cameras + 5 sensors (Apple). No public statement that any Vision Pro camera uses a focus actuator; the passthrough/tracking cameras are treated as fixed-focus in all coverage found. LG Innotek was reported as exclusive 3D-sensor supplier (KED Global, 2024-02-14).
- N50 glasses: two camera sensors in the current design — "one for photo and video capture and the other for computer vision tasks" (Gurman, 2026-07-26). The capture camera has "vertically-oriented oval-shaped lenses" (Gurman, 2026-05-31). As of July 2026 Apple had not finalised whether to ship a user-facing capture camera at all (three prototyped configurations). No report found that mentions autofocus, focus actuator type, or the camera-module vendor for N50. Reported chip: Apple-Watch-derived SoC built at TSMC (May 2025).
- Supplier signals: none naming the glasses camera module/actuator vendor. A search summary mentioned Foxconn/TSMC/Largan as engaged on the glasses, but this did not appear in any fetched article and is treated as unverified. SEMCO's Dec-2025 "global big tech" FP-coil deal (see Samsung cross-links) fits Apple's profile but is for smartphone AF/OIS actuators with "potential expansion to XR devices".
- Implication for the project: Apple's reported N50 timeline (prototype builds from late 2025; mass production Q2–mid 2027; ship late 2027) is compatible with a 2025–2026 supplier qualification program, but Apple's camera decision (capture vs. CV-only vs. none) was still open in mid-2026, which argues against a locked-in autofocus design-in at that date.

### 1.5 Cadence observations
- New-category announce-to-ship: Vision Pro 2023-06-05 -> 2024-02-02 = 242 days (~8 months); international wave +147 days (2024-06-28) and +161 days (2024-07-12). A WWDC-June-2027 N50 unveil with late-2027 shipment matches this ~6-month pattern.
- Refresh announce-to-ship: Vision Pro M5 2025-10-15 -> 2025-10-22 = 7 days (silicon swap only).
- OS cadence: WWDC (June 10, 2024 / June 9, 2025 / June 2026) -> release Sept 16, 2024 / Sept 15, 2025 / Sept 14, 2026, i.e. ~98 days, strictly annual.
- Glasses target drift (Gurman): end-2026 (2025-05-23) -> reveal 2026, ship 2027 (2025-10-01 and 2025-12-12) -> late 2027 (2026-05-31) -> WWDC-2027 unveil + late-2027 ship (2026-07-26) -> end-2027 (2026-09-27). Revisions arrive every 2–5 months; each slip has been 6–12 months. Kuo has been steadier: Q2-2027 mass production (June 2025) -> "2027" (June 2026).
- Component timing implied by reporting: "large quantities of prototypes" with overseas suppliers from late 2025 (Gurman) for a product now shipping late 2027 = roughly 24 months from EVT-scale builds to launch; mass production (Kuo: Q2 2027) ~2 quarters before shipment.

## 2. Google

### 2.1 Profile
Google is the platform owner of Android XR (announced 2024-12-12 with Samsung and Qualcomm) and a co-designer, but not the contract manufacturer, of the first Android XR glasses: Samsung builds the hardware (model codes SM-O200P/Warby Parker and SM-O200J/Gentle Monster) while Google supplies Gemini and the OS; Kering Eyewear (Gucci) is a 2027 partner; Xreal builds the tethered see-through Aura; Magic Leap supplies optics/reference designs for future display glasses. Google bought part of HTC's XR engineering team for US$250 million (2025-01-23) and committed up to US$150 million to Warby Parker (2025-05-20). Google's stated roadmap: audio/camera "AI glasses" launching "this fall" 2026 (Samsung official: November 2026), "display AI glasses" in 2027, Xreal Aura before end-2026. Relevance: Google is a U.S. "top-tier" OEM candidate in name, but the physical camera module in its first glasses is a Samsung design (single 12MP camera), so any U.S.-OEM-driven actuator qualification would more plausibly sit with Samsung's supply chain than Google's.

## 3. Samsung

### 3.1 Profile
Samsung Electronics (MX division) is the hardware lead of the Android XR alliance with Google and Qualcomm (collaboration dates from 2023 per CNBC). It shipped Galaxy XR (Project Moohan) on 2025-10-31 at US$1,799.99 (2x 6.5MP passthrough cameras, 6 tracking cameras, 4 eye-tracking cameras, Snapdragon XR2+ Gen 2), and on 2026-07-22 formally introduced display-less "intelligent eyewear" (Gentle Monster and Warby Parker editions; Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1; single camera; 9 h battery), which a Samsung official says launches in November 2026. Korean press codenames: "Haean" (first reported March 2025 as a 2025 product, now understood as the micro-LED display model for 2027) and "Jinju" (the display-free 2026 model). Samsung Electro-Mechanics (SEMCO), a Samsung group company, designs and makes camera lenses and actuators in-house and in Dec 2025 was reported close to its first actuator-component (FP coil) supply to an unnamed "global big tech". Relevance: Samsung is the actual maker of the first Google-ecosystem glasses, so the camera module and any focus actuator in those glasses come from Samsung's (likely SEMCO-centred) supply chain, and its exec statements set the clearest public dates among the three OEMs.
